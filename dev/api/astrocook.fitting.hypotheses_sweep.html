astrocook.fitting.hypotheses_sweep module#

The sweep of the anchored systems (phase 7, PR 7c.4d): the rarer and single lines, and the H I of the metal systems.

The second pass of the identification by hypotheses (astrocook.fitting.hypotheses, design/identification.md section 3.5). The anchors (the doublets and the pairs) find the systems; the other lines of a system are too weak, too rare or single (CII 1334, SiIII 1206, OI 1302, AlII 1670, the weaker FeII and SiII lines, the H I in the forest) to be found by a pair. For each metal system accepted by the first pass, and for each species of SWEEP_SPECIES with a line in the coverage other than that of the anchor, the hypothesis “Y at z”:

  • its components at the redshifts of the anchor’s components (one for the H I, at the column-weighted redshift of the anchor: the H I of a system is broader than its metal components and one line of it is what the forest resolves), N from the apparent optical depth at its predicted lines (the least over its lines: that of a line blended with the forest is an upper limit), z and b free; the components without influence on the pixels (evaluate_model(), min_influence 1) dropped after the first fit, the components that leave the profile of the anchor (_span()) make the hypothesis fail;

  • compared with the labels its pixels already have: the free lines of the default labels (the assumed Ly-a, the unidentified lines) that fall on its predicted lines are replaced by it, the other default components of the regions within its span (its neighbours: a line of the forest blended with one of its lines) are free on both sides, the rest of the model (every accepted component of the first pass and of the sweep so far, the components of the session) is fixed. Both sides get the short fit on the same pixels, without the refinement by the residuals of the first pass (the neighbours free do its work here; with it a test of SiII in the forest took minutes), and Y is accepted when its criterion is not above that of the labels by more than SWEEP_TIE;

  • an accepted species becomes an edge of the graph (from the region of the anchor’s strong line to the region of its strongest line), the regions it explains labelled with its lines, its components candidate systems with the weight of the comparison (exp(-dIC/2) normalised over the two).

What it does not do (design/identification.md): the upper limits where nothing is there (F156, 7d.7; the sweep skips a species with no absorption at its lines, SWEEP_SIGMA); the systems of the Ly-a/Ly-b pairs of the forest (their metals, the metals of the forest off by default: decision 2 of the design); the tellurics (7c.5).

astrocook.fitting.hypotheses_sweep.SWEEP_SIGMA = 3.0#

The least significance of the absorption at one of the predicted lines of a species (the sum of 1 - F over the profile of the anchor, in sigma of that sum) for it to be compared. Its purpose: the time; a species with nothing at its lines cannot lower the criterion (its components only add parameters), and its comparison is the upper limit of F156 (7d.7)

astrocook.fitting.hypotheses_sweep.SWEEP_TIE = 0.1#

“Y at z” is accepted when its criterion is not above that of the labels by more than this. Its purpose: where the H I of a system is the assumed Ly-a of a line whose Ly-b is not covered, the two models are the same (one line, the same three parameters) and their criteria differ by the noise of the two fits (about 1e-6); the tie goes to the label with a known redshift, the prior that makes a single line credible (design, section 3.5). 0.1: below any difference that the weights tell apart (0.49 against 0.51)

Type:

The tie of the comparison, in the units of the criterion

astrocook.fitting.hypotheses_sweep.SWEEP_INFLUENCE = 1.0#

The least influence of a component of “Y at z” (the chi2 change of removing it, as evaluate_model) to be kept after its first fit

astrocook.fitting.hypotheses_sweep.SWEEP_MAX_K = 24#

The largest comparison, in the free parameters of the labels of its pixels (the replaced components and the neighbours). Its purpose: the time. 24 (eight lines) when the Jacobian of the fit was by finite differences (F171: a test of SiII on a forest mock with 15 free lines took 5 minutes). Measured again with the analytic Jacobian (PR 7c.4d-bis, validate_sweep.py, sweep “all”, 36 mocks): 24, 36, 48 take 793, 3035, 7754 s to identify in all (the largest mock 172, 754, 3490 s) and find FeII 11, 15, 16 of 37, SiII 23, 30, 31 of 101, the rarer species accepted 282, 298, 303 (purity 0.81, 0.80, 0.80); the H I of the default sweep 42, 44, 46 of 120. Kept at 24 (Guido, 25 November 2026): a small gain at four times the time, and the rarer species need other strategies than the sweep (F174)
