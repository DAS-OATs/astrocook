Atomic Data#

Astrocook takes the rest wavelengths, oscillator strengths, damping constants and masses of the transitions from a single table, astrocook/data/atomic_data.ecsv. The fitter, the identification, the mock spectra and the GUI (redshift cursor, line markers, System Inspector) all use this table. This page describes its content and where each value comes from.

The table#

The table is an ECSV file, so astropy can read it with its units and the list of references:

from astropy.table import Table
from astrocook.core.atomic_data import ATOMIC_DATA_PATH

table = Table.read(ATOMIC_DATA_PATH, format="ascii.ecsv")
print(table.meta["references"])

Inside Astrocook the same values are available as ATOM_DATA (a dictionary keyed by transition) and xem_d (rest wavelengths as astropy quantities), both in astrocook.core.atomic_data.

The table has 211 transitions: the H I Lyman series up to Ly-15 and the Lyman limit, D I, the metal absorption lines of the intergalactic and interstellar medium, and some emission lines (used as markers, with f = 0).

A source key means that the value was taken from that source, or agrees with it within 2 mÅ in wavelength, 1% in f and 2% in gamma.

Choices and differences from other line lists#

The table was rebuilt in 2026 (issue #568). Each value was compared with Morton (2003), as distributed with linetools, and with the atom.dat of VPFIT. Some values were also compared with the line list of VoigtFit.

Newer values adopted instead of Morton (2003). They follow the atom.dat of VPFIT, and each one is checked against its original paper. The column “Before” gives the values of Astrocook 2.1, which are those of Morton (2003) except Ni II 1317, a line that Morton does not list:

Petitjean & Aracil (2004) measured the C IV wavelengths from high-redshift quasar absorbers, calibrated on Si IV. Their values agree with the laboratory wavelengths of Griesmann & Kling (2000, ApJ 536, L113, ADS).

Values of VPFIT not adopted, because their source is not clear:

  • the gamma of O I 971, 976, Mg I 2026, Mg II 2796, 2803, Co II 1466 and Fe I 3441, which differ from those of the table;

  • the placeholder gamma of 2e8 s⁻¹ for O II, O III and Fe II 1081;

  • S II 1253 at 1253.811 Å (Morton: 1253.805 Å);

  • the f of N I 1134.4149 (0.0278; Morton: 0.0287);

  • the values of Sn II 1400.

These choices only matter for the damping wings of very high columns, or for a wavelength shift of 1.4 km/s on S II 1253.

Corrections to earlier versions (Astrocook 2.1 and earlier):

  • The wavelengths of O III 832, P III 913, P V 1117 and Fe I 2501 were wrong by 0.2-0.7 Å, from a slip in the conversion from nm to Å.

  • The damping constant of Ar I 1066 was ten times too small.

  • The wavelength of Al II 1670 was 0.2 km/s off.

  • The damping constant of C I 1560 was 1.17e8 s⁻¹ instead of 1.27e8.

  • [Ne III] 3869 and [Ne V] 3346 were given in air instead of vacuum.

  • Not in the table, but in its use: the Voigt fit and the System Inspector computed the damping parameter of the profile with the observed wavelength of the line, a = γ λ (1 + z) / (4π b), instead of the rest one, a = γ λ / (4π b). The damping wings were (1 + z) times too strong, and a column density measured from them (damped Ly-α systems, Lyman-limit systems) came out too low by up to log(1 + z), 0.6 dex at z = 3. Astrocook 1 and the mock spectra used the rest wavelength.

  • The table listed a Ti II line at 3066.35 Å that no reference lists. It is kept with f = 0, so that sessions naming it still open, and the real Ti II 3067.24 Å line is added.

Transitions of Astrocook 1 not in the table. The V1 file also listed H₂ and CO bands, the Paschen and Brackett series, Si I, Balmer lines beyond Hε, and stellar and blended emission markers. Astrocook 2 never used them. They can be added when an analysis needs them.

Series and multiplets#

A series is written as a list of transitions (CIV_1548,CIV_1550) or by a name. Astrocook knows two kinds of names:

  • Standard multiplets (STANDARD_MULTIPLETS): the groups used by the identification and the automatic fits, strongest line first.

    • Doublets: CIV, SiIV, NV, OVI, MgII, AlIII, CII (with C II* 1335), ZnII, NaI, CaII.

    • Multiplets: NI, SiII, SII, CrII, MnII, FeII, NiII.

    • Hydrogen: Lyman (Ly-α to Ly-8), Ly_a, Ly_ab.

  • Series of the V1 syntax, used by the redshift cursor of the GUI and for older sessions. Each is the set of the transitions in the table with the same name before the underscore (e.g. MgII gives its six lines from 1025 to 2803 Å, Ly the whole Lyman series), in the order of the table. Their first transition is the reference of the redshift cursor: Ly-α for Ly, D and H. The aliases Ly-a, Ly-ab and Ly-abg give the first one, two or three Lyman lines.

A whole name is looked up first among the standard multiplets: MgII alone is the doublet, while in a list such as MgII;Ly_b it is the V1 series.
